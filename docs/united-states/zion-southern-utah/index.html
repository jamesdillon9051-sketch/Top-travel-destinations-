Welcome to Zion

Southern Utah holds five national parks — Zion, Bryce Canyon, Capitol Reef, Arches and Canyonlands — within a few hours' driving of each other, along with a state park system that would be national parks anywhere else. It is the densest concentration of spectacular landscape in North America, and the fact that you can drive between all five in a week is what makes it the best road trip in the country.

Zion is the busiest and the most immediately dramatic. The main canyon is a slot cut by the Virgin River through Navajo sandstone, with walls rising up to 600 metres almost vertically and, in the section called the Narrows, closing to about ten metres apart. Unlike the Grand Canyon, where you look down into the landscape, in Zion you stand at the bottom of it looking up — a completely different sensation.

The parks are geologically sequential, which is a genuinely useful thing to know. The rock layers exposed at the top of the Grand Canyon are the bottom layers at Zion, and Zion's top layers are Bryce's bottom ones. Driving north through the region is driving forward through about 250 million years of stacked deposition, and the guides at each park will explain their piece of it.

Why visit

Why visit Zion?

Nowhere else puts this much varied and accessible landscape in one place, and the variety between the parks is as striking as any of them individually.

  • The Narrows is a walk unlike any other

    You hike up the riverbed itself, in the water, between walls hundreds of metres high that narrow to about ten metres apart. It is the most memorable day hike in the American Southwest and requires no technical skill, only a stick and the right footwear.

  • Five parks, all different

    Zion is vertical red sandstone; Bryce is a natural amphitheatre of orange hoodoos at 2,400 m; Capitol Reef is a 160 km buckle in the earth's crust; Arches has over 2,000 natural stone spans; Canyonlands is mesas and gorges on a scale that swallows the eye.

  • The driving is the point

    Scenic Byway 12 between Bryce and Capitol Reef crosses a knife-edge ridge with drops on both sides and is regularly named among the best roads in the world. The Zion–Mount Carmel Highway climbs the canyon wall in switchbacks and a tunnel.

  • It is designed for hiking

    Trails at every level, from the paved Riverside Walk to Angels Landing's chain-assisted ridge. Almost everything is a day hike, and the parks are close enough that you can do a different one each morning.

  • The night skies are extraordinary

    Bryce Canyon has some of the darkest skies in the United States and runs an annual astronomy festival. On a clear moonless night you can see 7,500 stars with the naked eye — most places manage a few dozen.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Zion

Zion fills two or three days; the wider region needs a week. These are the fixed points of the classic loop.

  1. 01

    The Narrows

    A 26 km slot in the Virgin River, walked upstream from the end of the Riverside Walk. Most people go as far as Wall Street, about 3 km in, where the canyon closes to ten metres wide with 300-metre walls. You are in the water for most of it.

    Tip Rent neoprene socks, canyoneering boots and a walking stick from Springdale. Check the flash flood forecast — the canyon closes when the risk is high, and this is not a rule to test.

  2. 02

    Angels Landing

    A 450-metre climb ending on a narrow fin of rock with chains bolted into it and drops on both sides. Spectacular and genuinely exposed — several people have died from falls. A permit lottery now controls numbers on the final section.

    Tip Permits are allocated by seasonal and day-before lotteries. If you do not have one, Observation Point via East Mesa gives a higher view with no exposure.

  3. 03

    Bryce Canyon

    Not a canyon but an eroded amphitheatre on the edge of a plateau, filled with thousands of orange rock spires called hoodoos, formed by 200 freeze-thaw cycles a year. The rim sits at 2,400–2,700 m, so it is much cooler than Zion.

    Tip Walk down among the hoodoos on the Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden trail — about 5 km and the only way to grasp the scale. Sunrise Point at dawn is the classic view.

  4. 04

    Arches National Park

    Over 2,000 natural sandstone arches in a small area near Moab, including Delicate Arch — the one on the Utah number plate — reached by a 5 km round trip over slickrock.

    Tip Delicate Arch at sunset is the classic and the crowded option. Timed-entry reservations apply in peak season; check the current system before arriving.

  5. 05

    Canyonlands

    The largest Utah park, split into districts by the Green and Colorado rivers. Island in the Sky is the accessible one, a mesa with viewpoints over 300 metres of layered canyon. Mesa Arch at sunrise frames the light beneath it.

    Tip Mesa Arch at dawn is heavily photographed and correspondingly busy. Grand View Point at the end of the road is emptier and arguably better.

  6. 06

    Scenic Byway 12

    A 200 km road between Bryce and Capitol Reef, crossing the Grand Staircase-Escalante, climbing Boulder Mountain to 2,900 m and running along the Hogback — a ridge with sheer drops on both sides and no barrier.

    Tip Allow a full day with stops. Calf Creek Falls, a 10 km round trip to a 40-metre waterfall, breaks the drive well.

  7. 07

    Capitol Reef

    The quietest of the five, built around the Waterpocket Fold — a 160 km wrinkle in the earth's crust. It also holds the orchards of a Mormon settlement, where you can still pick fruit in season.

    Tip The scenic drive and the Hickman Bridge trail cover it in half a day. The pie at the historic Gifford Homestead is a genuine institution.

  8. 08

    Antelope Canyon and Horseshoe Bend

    Just over the Arizona border at Page. A slot canyon of sculpted sandstone accessible only on Navajo-guided tours, and a 300-metre-deep meander of the Colorado a short walk from the road.

    Tip Antelope Canyon cannot be visited independently. Book a Navajo-operated tour weeks ahead; midday gives the light beams in the upper canyon.

Things to do

Best things to do in Zion

This is a hiking and driving region. Almost everything worth doing involves one or the other, at either end of the day.

  1. Walk up the Narrows

    Take the shuttle to the Temple of Sinawava, walk the paved Riverside Walk, then step into the river. Most people turn around after two or three hours; the water is knee to waist deep depending on the season.

    Tip Water temperature is around 10°C most of the year. Rent proper boots and neoprene socks in Springdale — trainers are miserable.

  2. Descend into Bryce's amphitheatre

    The rim viewpoints are impressive; walking down among the hoodoos on the Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden is transformative. About 5 km with 200 m of climbing back out.

    Tip Do it clockwise, descending the steep Wall Street switchbacks and climbing the gentler Queen's Garden side.

  3. Drive Scenic Byway 12

    Two hundred kilometres between Bryce and Capitol Reef over slickrock desert, a 2,900 m mountain and a ridge with drops on both sides. One of the great drives anywhere.

    Tip Fuel is sparse. Fill up in Escalante or Boulder, and note that mobile coverage disappears entirely for long stretches.

  4. Watch the sky at Bryce

    One of the darkest skies in the country at 2,400 m with no light pollution. Rangers run astronomy programmes and the annual festival brings dozens of telescopes.

    Tip Take a warm layer — it can drop below freezing at night even in June.

  5. Get onto slickrock at Moab

    Arches and Canyonlands by day, and Moab in between as one of the world's great mountain-biking and four-wheel-drive towns. The Slickrock Trail is a rite of passage for cyclists.

    Tip Rafting the Colorado through the canyons is the low-effort alternative and runs from spring to autumn.

  6. Photograph at the right hour

    Red sandstone is flat and pale at midday and glows in the first and last hour of light. Delicate Arch at sunset, Mesa Arch at sunrise, Bryce's hoodoos at dawn, Zion's Watchman from the bridge at dusk.

    Tip The middle of the day is for driving between parks, not for looking at them.

Local food

What to eat in Zion

Southern Utah is small towns and park gateways, and the food is straightforward. The exceptions are worth knowing about because they are genuinely good and often the only option for fifty miles.

  • Utah scones and honey butter

    Not British scones but squares of fried dough served hot with honey butter, a Mormon pioneer inheritance found across the state and nowhere else.

    Where to try it Diners and family restaurants in the small towns between parks.

  • The Gifford Homestead pie

    Fruit pies baked at a preserved Mormon farmstead inside Capitol Reef, from orchards planted in the 1880s that visitors can still pick in season. Sold by the pie and usually gone by mid-morning.

    Where to try it Fruita, inside Capitol Reef National Park. Arrive early.

  • Navajo tacos

    Fry bread topped with beans, meat, cheese and salad, found across the Colorado Plateau and particularly on and around Navajo Nation towards Monument Valley.

    Where to try it Roadside stands and cafés between Moab, Monument Valley and Page.

  • Green chile everything

    Roasted Hatch chile in burgers, breakfast burritos and stews, spreading north from New Mexico into southern Utah. The default way to add flavour to road-trip food.

    Where to try it Diners in Moab, Springdale and Kanab. Best in autumn when the chile harvest arrives.

  • Springdale and Moab restaurants

    The two gateway towns have developed genuinely good restaurant strips serving hikers — hearty, seasonal and better than park-adjacent food usually is.

    Where to try it Springdale for Zion, Moab for Arches and Canyonlands. Both fill early in season.

  • Utah beer, and the rules

    Utah's alcohol laws are unusual: draught beer is capped at 5% ABV, spirits and stronger beer are sold only in state liquor stores, and restaurants have specific licensing rules.

    Where to try it Moab and Springdale both have breweries. Buy anything stronger at a state liquor store, closed on Sundays.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Zion

The region is a driving loop, so you will change base every two or three nights. Booking ahead matters enormously — the gateway towns are small and demand vastly exceeds supply in season.

  • Springdale

    A single street at the park gate, with the free shuttle running into the park and canyon walls rising directly above the hotels. Expensive and worth it, because you can be at the first shuttle.

    Best for: Zion

  • Moab

    The region's largest gateway town, an adventure-sports hub with the widest choice of accommodation, restaurants and outfitters. Both parks are within 40 minutes.

    Best for: Arches and Canyonlands

  • Bryce Canyon City and Tropic

    Small clusters at the park entrance and a few miles east. Limited but adequate, and being close matters because sunrise at the rim is the reason to be here.

    Best for: Bryce

  • Torrey

    A small town at the eastern end of Scenic Byway 12, with a couple of surprisingly good restaurants and dark skies. The natural overnight between Bryce and Moab.

    Best for: Capitol Reef and Byway 12

  • In-park lodges and campgrounds

    Zion Lodge, Bryce Canyon Lodge and the park campgrounds. All book out months ahead and all are worth the effort for being inside at dawn and dusk.

    Best for: Sunrise without driving

Transport

Getting to and around Zion

Las Vegas is the usual gateway for a southern Utah loop and Salt Lake City the alternative. Either way this is a driving trip.

Getting there

  • Las Vegas (LAS)

    About 2h30 to Zion and the standard start of the loop, with cheap flights, cheap car hire and a straightforward drive northeast on Interstate 15.

    Also the natural end point, since the loop closes back towards Las Vegas from the Grand Canyon.

  • Salt Lake City (SLC)

    About 4h30 to Zion and 4h to Moab, with more domestic connections and often cheaper fares than Las Vegas.

    Better if you are doing the loop in the other direction, starting with Arches and Canyonlands.

  • St George and Cedar City

    Small regional airports within an hour of Zion, with limited connections through Salt Lake City or Phoenix.

    Convenient if the schedules work, but usually more expensive than driving from Las Vegas.

  • Combining with Arizona

    Page and Antelope Canyon are 2h from Bryce, Monument Valley 2h30 from Moab, and the Grand Canyon 2h30 from Page.

    The full loop of Utah and northern Arizona takes 12 to 14 days and is the best road trip in the country.

Getting around

A car for the region, and the park shuttle inside Zion Canyon, which is closed to private vehicles for most of the year.

  • Rental car

    Essential. Distances between parks run two to three hours and there is no public transport at all between them. A standard car handles every paved road in all five parks.

    Fuel stations are sparse on Scenic Byway 12 and in the Grand Staircase. Fill up whenever you drop below half.

  • The Zion shuttle

    Free, mandatory, and the only way up Zion Canyon from roughly March to November — private cars are barred. Runs frequently from the visitor centre with nine stops up the canyon.

    A second free shuttle runs through Springdale to the park entrance, so you can leave the car at the hotel entirely.

  • Timed entry and permits

    Arches operates timed-entry reservations in peak season, and Zion runs a permit lottery for Angels Landing. Both change annually.

    Check each park's own page as soon as your dates are fixed. These are the bookings that shape the trip.

  • Walking

    Everything worth seeing is a hike, from paved half-hour walks to full-day routes. Trails are well marked and the parks publish honest difficulty ratings.

    Slickrock is disorienting — routes are marked with stone cairns rather than paths. Follow them carefully.

  • Guided tours

    Required for Antelope Canyon, which is on Navajo land, and useful for canyoneering, rafting and four-wheel-drive routes around Moab.

    Antelope Canyon tours book out weeks ahead in season.

When to go

Best time to visit Zion

The desert parks are unbearable in high summer and the higher ones can be snowbound in winter. The shoulder seasons are not just better, they are a different trip.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Best time

    Warm at 18–27°C, wildflowers out, and the Virgin River high enough to be interesting without closing the Narrows. Bryce at 2,400 m is still cold and may hold snow into April.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Think twice

    Zion and Moab routinely exceed 38°C and hiking after 10:00 becomes dangerous. July and August also bring monsoon thunderstorms and flash flood risk, which closes the Narrows and the slot canyons. Bryce, being higher, stays pleasant.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    The pick of the year. Temperatures fall to the low twenties, the crowds thin after the Labor Day weekend, cottonwoods turn yellow along the Virgin River, and the light is at its best.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Quiet

    Cold and nearly empty, with Zion's shuttle suspended so you can drive the canyon yourself. Bryce with snow on orange hoodoos is one of the great American sights. Some roads and trails close, and the Narrows is generally out.

Worth planning around: Flash flood risk from July to September. Slot canyons including the Narrows and Antelope flood from rain falling many kilometres away, with no warning at the canyon itself. Check the daily forecast and obey closures without exception.

Itinerary

Seven days in southern Utah

A one-way loop from Las Vegas, hitting four parks and the best road in the country, with no leg over three hours.

  1. Day1

    Las Vegas to Zion

    Collect the car and get to the canyon.

    • Morning Fly in, pick up the car, drive northeast — about 2h30 to Springdale.
    • Afternoon The shuttle up Zion Canyon and the paved Riverside Walk to the mouth of the Narrows.
    • Evening The Watchman from the footbridge at sunset.
  2. Day2

    Zion

    The big Zion day, starting at the first shuttle.

    • Morning The Narrows — three hours up the river to Wall Street and back, in rented boots.
    • Afternoon Angels Landing if you have a permit, or the Watchman Trail for a lower-effort view.
    • Evening Springdale, and an early night.
  3. Day3

    Zion to Bryce

    Over the canyon wall and up 1,000 metres.

    • Morning The Zion–Mount Carmel Highway east, through the 1930 tunnel and the switchbacks.
    • Afternoon Bryce — the rim viewpoints, then the Navajo Loop and Queen's Garden among the hoodoos.
    • Evening Sunset Point, then stargazing at 2,400 m.
  4. Day4

    Scenic Byway 12 to Torrey

    The drive that justifies the whole trip.

    • Morning Sunrise at Bryce, then east on Byway 12 across the Grand Staircase.
    • Afternoon Calf Creek Falls, then the Hogback ridge and over Boulder Mountain.
    • Evening Torrey, with dark skies and a surprisingly good dinner.
  5. Day5

    Capitol Reef to Moab

    The quietest park, then across to the busiest corner.

    • Morning Capitol Reef's scenic drive, Hickman Bridge and pie at the Gifford Homestead.
    • Afternoon Drive southeast to Moab, about 2h20.
    • Evening Arches for Delicate Arch at sunset — a 5 km round trip over slickrock.
  6. Day6

    Canyonlands and Arches

    Sunrise on a mesa, and the rest of Arches.

    • Morning Island in the Sky before dawn — Mesa Arch, then Grand View Point.
    • Afternoon Dead Horse Point, or rafting the Colorado, or the rest of Arches.
    • Evening Moab.
  7. Day7

    South, or back

    Continue the loop or close it.

    • Morning Drive south to Monument Valley, about 2h30, and on towards the Grand Canyon.
    • Afternoon Alternatively, return west towards Las Vegas — a long day of about 6h.
    • Evening Onward, or the flight home.

Travel tips

Zion travel tips

Several of these are safety items in a landscape that is less forgiving than it looks.

  • Flash floods are the real danger here. Slot canyons including the Narrows and Antelope flood from rain falling many kilometres upstream, with no sign of weather where you are standing. Check the daily flood forecast and obey closures absolutely.
  • Angels Landing requires a permit allocated by seasonal and day-before lotteries. Without one you cannot climb the final chained section — and Observation Point via East Mesa gives a higher view with no exposure at all.
  • Rent proper Narrows gear in Springdale. Canyoneering boots, neoprene socks and a stick cost about $25–55 and turn a miserable slippery wade into a genuine pleasure. The water sits at around 10°C most of the year.
  • Arches now operates timed-entry reservations in peak season and Zion's canyon is closed to private cars for most of the year. Check each park's own current system as soon as your dates are set.
  • Carry at least four litres of water per person per day and start hikes at dawn. Summer temperatures in Zion and Moab exceed 38°C, and heat illness is the most common serious incident in these parks.
  • Bryce sits at 2,400–2,700 m, roughly 1,500 m higher than Zion. It can be below freezing at night in June while Zion is 30°C. Pack for both.
  • Fuel stations are sparse on Scenic Byway 12 and across the Grand Staircase, and mobile coverage disappears for long stretches. Fill up whenever you drop below half a tank and download offline maps.
  • Utah's alcohol laws are unusual — draught beer is capped at 5%, and anything stronger comes from a state liquor store, which is closed on Sundays.

FAQ

Zion: frequently asked questions

Do you need a permit for Angels Landing?

For the final chained section, yes. Zion introduced a permit lottery in 2022 to control numbers on the exposed ridge, with seasonal advance lotteries and a day-before lottery. The rest of the trail to Scout Lookout needs no permit. If you miss out, Observation Point reached via the East Mesa trail gives a higher and arguably better view with none of the exposure.

How hard is hiking the Narrows?

Not technically difficult, but physically awkward — you walk up the riverbed itself in water that ranges from ankle to waist deep, over slippery cobbles. Most people go about 3 km to Wall Street and back, which takes three to four hours. Rent canyoneering boots, neoprene socks and a walking stick in Springdale; the water sits around 10°C most of the year and trainers are miserable.

How many days do you need for southern Utah?

Two or three for Zion alone; six to nine for the region. A seven-day loop from Las Vegas covers Zion, Bryce, Scenic Byway 12, Capitol Reef, Arches and Canyonlands without any leg over three hours. Extending to twelve or fourteen days lets you add Monument Valley, Antelope Canyon and the Grand Canyon.

Can you drive in Zion Canyon?

Not for most of the year. From roughly March to November the scenic drive up the main canyon is closed to private vehicles and served by a free mandatory shuttle from the visitor centre, running frequently to nine stops. In winter the shuttle is suspended and you can drive it yourself, which is one of the quiet pleasures of an off-season visit.

When is the best time to visit Zion and southern Utah?

April to May and September to October. Summer temperatures in Zion and Moab exceed 38°C and bring monsoon flash-flood risk that closes the Narrows and slot canyons. Winter is cold and nearly empty, with Bryce spectacular under snow. Note that Bryce sits 1,500 m higher than Zion and can be freezing while Zion is warm.

Which southern Utah park is the best?

They are genuinely different rather than better or worse. Zion for vertical red canyon walls and the Narrows; Bryce for the hoodoo amphitheatre and the dark skies; Arches for the sandstone spans; Canyonlands for the sheer scale; Capitol Reef for the quiet. If you can only do two, Zion and Bryce are two hours apart and the strongest pairing.

How dangerous are flash floods?

They are the most serious risk in this landscape and they kill people. Slot canyons including the Narrows and Antelope flood from rainfall many kilometres upstream, so the sky above you can be clear when the water arrives. The parks publish a daily flash flood potential rating and close canyons when it is high — those closures are not advisory and should never be tested.
